🗂️ Comprehensive guide to value-added tax in Saudi Arabia

What is value added tax?

VAT is an indirect tax imposed on all goods and services. It was implemented in Saudi Arabia in 2018 at a rate of 5%, then raised to 15% on July 1, 2020. It is managed by the Zakat, Tax and Customs Authority.

How to add tax

Tax value = amount x 0.15

Amount after tax = Amount x 1.15

📝 example: Amount of 1,000 riyals + tax = 1,150 riyals

How to remove the tax

Amount before tax = Amount ÷ 1.15

📝 example: The amount of 1,150 riyals including tax = 1,000 riyals before tax
💡 advice: To quickly find the price before tax, divide by 1.15.

Official references: Zakat, Tax and Customs Authority (ZATCA.gov.sa) | Ministry of Finance (mof.gov.sa)

VAT Calculator in Saudi Arabia 2026: Your comprehensive guide to an accurate calculation compatible with the Zakat, Tax and Customs Authority

As the tax base in the Kingdom of Saudi Arabia continues to expand and the number of establishments registered for VAT rises to more than 700,000 by 2026, the need for an accurate calculation tool has become more urgent than ever. Whether you are an online store owner, a provider of consulting services, or a merchant in the retail sector, a mistake in calculating the tax may expose you to fines of up to 50 thousand riyals, according to the Saudi value-added tax system. Here the importance of the “VAT Calculator” is highlighted as a practical tool that saves you time and gives you immediate, reliable results.

How does the step-by-step VAT calculator work?

The VAT calculator is based on simple but accurate mathematical equations, and allows you to add the tax to the base price or extract it from a price that includes it. This dual function comes in line with what is stipulated in the executive regulations of the value-added tax system issued by the Zakat, Tax and Customs Authority, where taxpayers are required to show the value of the tax separately in tax invoices.

To use the calculator correctly, follow these steps:

  1. Select the type of operation: Do you want to add the tax to the price of the item (base calculation), or extract the tax value from a comprehensive price (reverse calculation)?
  2. Enter the amount: Write the value of the amount in Saudi Riyals without commas or symbols.
  3. Choose the tax rate: The standard percentage in Saudi Arabia is 15%, and there is a zero percentage (0%) for some exported goods and services, in addition to cases of complete exemption.
  4. Click on the Account button: The calculator will show you three numbers: the price before tax, the tax value, and the final price including tax.
  5. Check the result: Review the numbers before including them in your electronic tax invoice via the Authority’s “Fatora” platform.

It should be noted that the calculator does not replace understanding the regulations, as the Saudi taxpayer must commit to submitting tax returns every three months if his revenues exceed 40 million riyals, or monthly if they exceed 100 million riyals, as determined by the Zakat, Tax and Customs Authority.

A practical example from the Saudi market

Suppose you are the owner of a small business in Riyadh that sells electronic devices, and you sold a laptop for 4,500 riyals before tax. When you enter this amount into the calculator and choose 15%, the result will appear as follows:

  • Price before tax: 4,500 riyals
  • VAT value: 675 riyals
  • Final price including tax: 5,175 riyals

If you have a total bill of 2,300 riyals, including tax, and you want to know the hidden tax value, the calculator will divide the amount by 1.15, showing you the tax value of 300 riyals and the original price of 2,000 riyals.

Three important tips for the Saudi user

  • Keep your records for at least 6 years: The Saudi value-added tax system requires taxpayers to keep invoices and accounting records for a period of no less than 6 years, to avoid fines upon audit.
  • Make sure you are registered for VAT if your revenue exceeds 375 thousand riyals: Registration is mandatory upon reaching this limit, and optional between 187,500 and 375 thousand riyals, according to the authority.
  • Use approved electronic invoicing: All tax invoices must be issued through systems compatible with the requirements of the Zakat, Tax and Customs Authority, showing the tax number and quick response (QR) code.

Frequently asked questions about the VAT calculator

Can I use the calculator for exempt items?
Yes, but you must choose a rate of 0% or specify the exemption status manually, knowing that some sectors such as private education and health are eligible for a zero rate under specific conditions.

Does the ratio differ between goods and services?
No, the standard rate of 15% applies to most goods and services, except for special cases specified in the executive regulations.

Is the calculator officially approved by the Zakat, Tax and Customs Authority?
Electronic calculators are helpful tools for quick calculations, but official approval is through the tax return submitted on the Authority’s portal, so it is recommended to always match the results with your accounting system.
